Module 3 — Design Thinking

Definition. A human-centric approach to innovation that starts from what real users need, not from what the company can build. It is iterative and non-linear.

Names to know: popularised by IDEO and its CEO Tim Brown (Change by Design, 2019 — on your syllabus). The five-stage model comes from Stanford’s d.school.

The five stages — most likely question on the paper

Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test
StageWhat happensOutput
1. EmpathiseUnderstand users — observe, interview, immerse. No solutions yet.User insights
2. DefineFrame the real problemProblem statement / POV
3. IdeateGenerate many ideas — brainstorming, SCAMPERA pile of ideas
4. PrototypeBuild a quick, cheap, scaled-down versionA testable model
5. TestUsers try it, feedback gathered, then loop backRefinements
True / False trap “Design Thinking is a strictly linear process” → FALSE. You may return to any earlier stage at any time.

The three lenses

A good solution sits where all three overlap: Desirability (do people want it? — human) · Feasibility (can we build it? — technical) · Viability (can it sustain itself? — business).

Divergent vs Convergent thinking

Divergent — open up, generate many options (Ideate). Convergent — narrow down, select the best (Define, and after Ideate). Design Thinking alternates between the two.

Empathy mapping — four quadrants

SAYS · THINKS · DOES · FEELS

Says and Does are observable. Thinks and Feels must be inferred — you cannot see a thought. (Frequently asked.) Other Empathise-stage tools: user interviews, observation, personas, journey maps.

Problem framing (Define stage)

  • POV statement: [User] needs [need] because [insight]
  • “How Might We…” (HMW) — reframes a problem as an opportunity
  • A good problem statement is human-centred, broad enough for creativity, narrow enough to act on

Brainstorming rules

Defer judgement · Go for quantity · Encourage wild ideas · Build on others’ ideas · Stay on topic · One conversation at a time · Be visual.

True / False trap “Weak ideas should be criticised immediately during brainstorming” → FALSE. Judgement is deferred; quantity beats quality at this stage.

Prototyping & testing

  • Low-fidelity = rough, cheap, fast (paper, cardboard). High-fidelity = close to the real product.
  • Purpose: fail fast, fail cheap — learn before spending money
  • Iterative feedback loop: build → test → refine → repeat

Benefits: user satisfaction, reduced risk, encourages innovation, cross-functional teamwork. Limitations: time-consuming, needs skilled facilitation, results hard to measure.

Module 4 — Technology, Transformation & Industry 4.0

The four industrial revolutions

EraDriven by
Industry 1.0~1784Steam power, water power, mechanisation
Industry 2.0~1870Electricity, mass production, the assembly line
Industry 3.0~1969Electronics, computers, IT, basic automation
Industry 4.02011 →Cyber-physical systems, IoT, AI, cloud

The term “Industry 4.0” was coined in Germany in 2011 (Hannover Fair). Industry 5.0 is the emerging next step — human–machine collaboration, sustainability, personalisation.

The nine pillars of Industry 4.0

Big Data & Analytics · Autonomous Robots · Simulation (Digital Twin) · System Integration · Industrial IoT · Cybersecurity · Cloud Computing · Additive Manufacturing (3D printing) · Augmented Reality

AR vs VR

AR = your PHONE. Camera up, you see your actual room with something added (Pokémon Go, Instagram filters). Reality is still there.
VR = a HEADSET over your eyes. Your room is gone, replaced by a virtual world.
Phone → AR. Headset → VR. If you can still see the real world, it is AR.

Deployment models: public · private · hybrid.

Easy to get wrong Gmail is SaaS, not PaaS. The “platform” in PaaS is a development platform for building your own software — not the everyday sense in which Gmail is “a platform”. SaaS also has nothing to do with subscriptions: Gmail is free and still SaaS.

Pizza version: IaaS = frozen pizza in your oven · PaaS = delivery to your table · SaaS = you go to a restaurant.

Digital transformation

Integrating digital technology into all areas of a business, fundamentally changing how it operates and delivers value. A digital roadmap is the phased plan for getting there.

True / False trap “Digital transformation means simply purchasing new software” → FALSE. It requires culture, process and business-model change too.

Sustainable operations

  • Triple Bottom Line: People · Planet · Profit (the 3 Ps)
  • Circular economy — reduce, reuse, recycle; design out waste. Opposite of the linear “take–make–dispose” model
  • ESG — Environmental, Social, Governance
  • SDGs — UN Sustainable Development Goals (17)
  • Green computing — energy-efficient IT

Ethical issues in digital services

Data privacy (GDPR) · algorithmic bias · job displacement through automation · transparency and accountability · cybersecurity and consent.

The five you actually got wrong tonight

  1. Digital Twin = a live virtual replica of a physical thing, not a cloned database.
  2. Gmail / Netflix / Zoom = SaaS, not PaaS. You are an end user, not a developer.
  3. AR adds to reality (phone). VR replaces it (headset).
  4. IoT = Internet of Things — physical objects, not “technology”.
  5. Empathy map: Thinks and Feels are inferred; Says and Does are observed. (True, not false.)
